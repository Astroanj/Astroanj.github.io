## 🌌 About Me
I am an observational astrophysicist specialising in the discovery, kinematics, and evolution of **low-mass stars**, **brown dwarfs**, and **Ultra-Compact Dwarfs (UCDs)**. My research uses massive, multi-mission space-based datasets to explore the structural components of the local universe and map the low-mass end of the Initial Mass Function (IMF).

I am originally from Kerala, in southern India. I pursued my doctoral degree at the historic Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Padova (UniPD) in Italy, where Galileo Galilei once taught! During my tenure as a PhD student, I investigated binaries and associated stellar populations in the globular clusters belonging to our Galaxy and beyond. Currently, as a postdoctoral researcher in France, I work with both the **Euclid Consortium** and the **Gaia collaboration**, leveraging the powerful multi-wavelength synergies of European flagship space missions to drive automated data-mining pipelines.

---

## 🪐 Core Research Focus
* **Substellar Populations:** Sifting high-volume archival catalogues to extract hidden stellar and substellar objects.

  
* **Stellar Multiplicity & Clusters:** Analysing binary fractions and evolutionary benchmarks inside coeval star clusters.
  Globular clusters are one of the oldest and densest systems, and they host a substantial number of binary stars and exotic objects, many of which have evolutionary links with binaries. Using high-precision photometry and astrometry obtained from the Hubble Space Telescope, I have explored the fraction and distribution of binaries and related stellar populations in different star clusters. Their fraction and distribution were explored as a function of various stellar and cluster parameters for potential relations,  which will improve our understanding of their formation and evolution, as well as the dynamics underway in the clusters.
  
* **Cross-Mission Pipelines:** Constructing unified optical-astrometry and near-infrared (NIR) data selection frameworks.
